Two families, and which is which

The app already has a line-icon set in Icon.tsx, matched path for path to the boards. It stays. Spots are a second family, for a different job, and the two never stand in for each other.

Glyphs — actions and labels

One colour, a 1.7–2.3 stroke, 15–21 pt. In rows, bars, buttons, beside a figure. Unchanged.

Spots — places and games

Full colour, 32–96 pt. On a card or tile that opens a place, at the head of a game’s description, in an empty state.

The material

Everything is assembled from the same few parts in art.mjs — a chip, a stack, a chip on its edge, a card, a bomb, a flame — so a new drawing inherits the projection and the colours instead of guessing them.

  1. Top faceonly the top chip shows one
  2. Inlay ringa lighter line at 0.6 r
  3. Rim dasheseight, lined up with the inserts
  4. Edge insertseight round the edge, each as wide as the gap
  5. Seama dark hairline where two chips meet
  6. Ground shadowone soft ellipse, never a drop shadow

Every spot is drawn on a 96 × 96 box with the ground at 84, and shown through an 88 × 88 window from (4, 12). Flat things are ellipses at 0.34; standing things are face-on. The light is always upper left, which is why a chip’s side is the darker colour.

The rules

Short enough to hold in your head while drawing the next one.

Only things that sit on a poker table.

Chips, cards, the dealer button, a bomb, the book. No people, no hands, no abstract shapes. If it couldn’t be put down on the felt, it isn’t a spot.

One projection.

Seen from a little above: a circle lying flat is an ellipse 0.34 as tall as it is wide. Things that stand up (cards, the book, a chip on its edge) are drawn face-on. Everything stands on one ground line with one soft shadow under it.

Three materials, and fire.

Ink, felt red, bone. Card stock is bone. Amber appears only as a flame or a spark, so the two places it shows — Boosters and the bomb — read as the exciting ones.

No outlines.

An edge is where two materials meet. The only strokes are the seam between chips, the inlay ring, and a card’s hairline edge so it holds on white.

The inserts are the signature.

Every chip has eight, each exactly as wide as the gap between them, and they follow the edge’s curve. That stripe is what makes something read as ours at 32 pt.

Flat fills, light from the upper left.

The side of a chip is darker than its face; that is the only shading. No gradients — it keeps the drawings portable to react-native-svg as they are.

Below 44 pt, drop the fine detail.

The rim dashes, the inlay ring, letters and card indices go. The silhouette, the inserts and the colours stay.

Dark mode moves one colour.

Ink lifts from #232837 to #363D52 so a black chip separates from a dark card. Red, bone and fire do not move; the shadow deepens.

Spots name places and games; glyphs do everything else.

A spot sits on a card or a tile that opens somewhere. Inside a row, a bar, a button or beside a figure, it is a line glyph from Icon.tsx — never a spot, and never a spot shrunk down to glyph size.
