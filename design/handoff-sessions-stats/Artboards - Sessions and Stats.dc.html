The graph

Tapping a night, and the nights at the edges

Selection is drawn the same way every time: the tapped bar keeps full colour, the other seven drop to a third, its date label goes white, and its slice of the baseline brightens. The figure appears in the header, where the “result per night” caption used to sit.

Bar height · the rule

One scale for both halves, set by the biggest night in the window

Take the eight nights on screen. Find the single largest magnitude among them — best win or worst loss, whichever is bigger in absolute terms. That night fills its half of the plot; every other bar is drawn at the same dollars-per-pixel. Wins and losses therefore share one scale and stay comparable, and the plot re-scales whenever the timeframe changes.

Floor. A night never draws thinner than 3px, so a $6 result on this window (0.4px unclamped) still reads. A night that finished at exactly zero straddles the baseline instead: 2px of yellow above the line and 2px below, and the baseline itself left alone. Never nothing, and never green or red. It keeps full yellow even when another night is tapped — 4px of mark cannot survive being dimmed — and it carries a $0 resting straight on the mark, set at the same 14px as the header readout, because a zero night has no height to read. Cap. Nothing exceeds 38px, so no bar can reach the header above or the date labels below. All-win or all-loss windows. The empty half stays empty; the baseline does not move to the middle of the used half. The graph reads as one-sided because the record is one-sided. Fewer than eight nights. Columns keep their width and the row is left-aligned; the peak comes from whatever nights exist, so a two-night window still has one full-height bar. Outliers. A single huge night flattens the rest by design — that is the honest reading, and the tapped-night figure covers the detail the flattening hides.
The readout timeline · 3a

The figure holds for 2.75s after the last tap, then fades

Selection is transient. The figure, the bar highlight, the white date label and the brightened baseline slice are one state with one timer — they arrive together and leave together, so the graph is never left holding a highlight with no number next to it. The clock counts from the last tap, not the first.

0msOn touch down the tapped bar goes to full colour, its date label to white, its baseline slice to 28% white. The header caption “result per night” cross-fades to the figure over 120ms, ease-out. No movement, no scale, no bounce — the card height is fixed.
0 → 2750msHold the figure stays fully opaque. Every new tap resets this timer to zero. Tapping a different bar swaps the figure in place with a 120ms cross-fade and moves the highlight; nothing fades out in between.
2750 → 3150msFade out figure and highlight fade together over 400ms, ease-in. Bars return to 34% alpha, the date label to muted, the baseline to its hairline, and the caption cross-fades back in on the same 400ms.
InterruptsLeaving the state a scroll of more than 8px, a tap anywhere outside the plot, a change of group or period, or navigating away clears the state immediately with the same 400ms fade — no hold.
Nothing else reacts. The period total above the graph, Last games and the extremes are unaffected by a tap. The tap is a read, not a filter. Reduced motion. Both cross-fades collapse to instant swaps; the 2750ms hold is unchanged. Repeat taps on the same bar reset the timer and do not re-animate the figure. Haptic. One light selection tick per bar change, none on a repeat tap of the same bar.
