1 · The decisions this builds on

From the owner, 20 October, and from the research the week before:

  1. Inside the ledger app, not a separate app. The research found no audience for standalone rules apps (0–22 ratings across the category), no willingness to pay for rules text, and a ledger that already holds the three things a ruling needs: the players, the stakes and the host.
  2. A default list of verified answers, written by us. The app ships the list; the list is the product.
  3. AI for anything the list misses, visibly marked as AI. The AI answer is never presented as ours.
  4. Every miss is captured for review, so the list grows from what people actually ask rather than from what we imagine they ask.
  5. Cash game first. Every answer is written for a cash game. Where a tournament ruling differs, the answer says so in a note that cannot be mistaken for the answer.
  6. Navigation is the hard part. Seven areas, sixty situations, a host with one thumb and a table waiting.
  7. Reference only. (22 October.) A ruling is read at the table and that is the end of it: nothing is written to the night, the log or the settled screen. The ledger stays money. What the app keeps is the question, for the review queue, never the decision.
  8. Verified and AI answers are both badged, in the app's own pill: a check and VERIFIED on ours, AI in the amber the app already uses for anything waiting on somebody.

Two constraints from the research that shape the whole thing:

  • Robert's Rules of Poker cannot be bundled. Its private-game edition bars commercial use without the late author's consent. Every answer here is written in our words, and cites Robert's Rules or the TDA rules as source, never quotes them at length.
  • General chatbots fail on exactly the procedural parts — pot counting, straddles, the five-card rule (Silver, May 2025). So the AI never does arithmetic. Side pots, rebuy caps, rounding and the short bank are computed in packages/core, which is the only place arithmetic happens in this app and the only place it is tested. The AI explains; the engine counts.

2 · Where it lives

Three entry points, one library, two chromes. The pattern already exists in the app: Money rules is a push from Settings (the group's defaults, a place) and a sheet from Tonight (tonight's list, one thing to do). Rulings follow it exactly, which means nothing new for a host to learn about which gesture gets them back.

2.2 The three entry points

Settings. A new Action row after Money rules: Rules & rulings (UNSURE — label). It pushes /rules. This is where a host reads before a night, sets the group's table rules, and shares the printable sheet.

The table-admin drawer on Tonight. A fifth row, Rulings (UNSURE — label; icon not drawn), between Money rules and Pass the game. It opens the Rulings sheet. The drawer is "the dock expanding in place" and does not join the navigation stack, so this costs nothing in depth. Only the phone recording the night sees the drawer; a watcher gets the same library from Settings.

Count up. When In play and Accounted for disagree, the discrepancy is already the headline. One row beneath the counted list — If the bank is short (UNSURE) — opens the short-bank situation directly, resolved against the group's rule. The count-up header is owned by the 6 September cut and this row must sit outside the block the cut draws. Whether it should exist at all is in § 12.

2.3 What is deliberately not an entry point

  • Not a dock button. The dock has three slots and they are money.
  • Not a tab. There is no tab bar.
  • Not the club home. The root is the night and the book. A host who wants rules before a night goes to Settings, the same way they go for Money rules.
  • Not a sheet over a player card. A ruling is about the table, not a player, and the player card already raises the amount sheet, which is the floor.

3 · Finding a situation in three taps

The research is specific about what a host needs at the table: the dozen judgement calls that start arguments (mucked winner, verbal versus chips, string bet, out of turn, exposed card) and the dozen money-procedure questions that rulebooks never cover (short bank, rebuy cap, going south, leaving). Lookups — who wins, kickers, blinds — are asked a hundred times more often but by beginners, usually before the night and usually of Google. So the two surfaces are weighted differently.

Every string in the mock is UNSURE. The structure is the decision:

  • Search first, because a host who knows what happened types two words. Search runs offline over title, aliases and keywords (§ 4), ranks by prefix match then substring, and shows at most six rows. A phrase that matches nothing shows the Ask row alone.
  • Twelve situations, by name of what happened, not by rule. "Winning hand hit the muck", not "Dead hands". The host is describing, not classifying. These twelve are the research's ranking (§ 9 marks them ★) and the list is fixed per content edition, not personalised — a host should find the same row in the same place every week.
  • Areas as a chip row underneath, for the long tail. Tapping an area replaces the twelve with that area's list; the sheet's search and close stay.
  • The sub-line names the edition and that the group's rules apply. A ruling read from this sheet is dated by it.

3.2 The Rules push (from Settings)

The same library as a place. Search at the top; then the seven areas as sections, each a captioned list of its situations in the order § 9 gives; then Your rules (UNSURE) as the last section, with the parameters the group has set and the ones still at default, and a Share the rules sheet row (UNSURE) that produces the one-page printable the research found hosts search for ("poker rules printable", "poker cash game house rules").

Decisions in the sheet:

  • The badge is in the title row, where the app puts every state: the Pill component, plain tone, a check glyph and VERIFIED (UNSURE — label). Plain, not green: the token file is explicit that if something in this app is coloured, it is money. The AI answer's pill is the same component in the amber tone (§ 6.4), so the two states sit in the same place and differ in colour and word, and a host can tell them apart across the table.
  • The answer is the body, plain prose, at most three short paragraphs. No bullets in an answer: the host reads it aloud.
  • Your group is the house-rule binding (§ 5): the parameter this situation depends on, with its value, and Change which goes to Table rules. When the parameter is unset, the row reads the default and says Not set for your group (UNSURE) so a host knows the answer is ours, not theirs.
  • Source is a citation, never a quote (§ 1).
  • Tournament is a collapsed row, muted, and only present when the ruling differs. Expanded, it is one or two sentences in the same muted colour under a TOURNAMENT caption. The cash answer stays the only text at body weight on the screen. When the ruling is the same the row is absent — saying "same in a tournament" sixty times teaches people to stop reading the row.
  • No primary button. The sheet ends at Helpful?; there is nothing to save (§ 8). A host reads it and swipes it away.
  • Helpful? feeds the review queue (§ 7). A 👎 on a verified answer is the strongest signal we get.

4 · The content model

The library ships inside the app as data, versioned by edition, so it works at a kitchen table with no signal and so a ruling can say which edition it was read from. One file, packages/core/src/rulings/situations.json (path UNSURE — wherever the engine's own data lives), typed and linted by a test.

interface Situation {
  id: string;                 // 'showdown.mucked-winner' — stable for ever; rows are keyed by it
  area: Area;                 // 'betting' | 'showdown' | 'hands' | 'dealing' | 'money' | 'behaviour'
  atTheTable: boolean;        // one of the twelve on the Rulings sheet
  order: number;              // position within its area
  title: string;              // what happened, as a player says it
  aliases: string[];          // other ways people say it — search reads these
  keywords: string[];         // the words search matches on
  cash: Answer;               // the ruling, for a cash game — always present
  tournament?: {              // present only when the ruling differs
    note: string;             // one or two sentences
    source: Citation[];
  };
  binds?: TableRuleKey[];     // the group parameters the answer reads (§ 5)
  computes?: 'sidePots' | 'shortBank' | 'rebuyCap';   // hands the numbers to the engine
  related: string[];          // ids
  status: 'verified' | 'draft';
  verified?: { on: string; by: string };   // ISO date; a name, for the file, not a screen
}

interface Answer {
  body: string;               // ≤ 3 paragraphs; may contain {{rule.key}} placeholders
  source: Citation[];
}

interface Citation {
  work: 'roberts' | 'tda' | 'house';
  ref: string;                // 'Showdown', 'r.15', or the TableRuleKey for 'house'
}

Rules of the file:

  • id never changes and is never reused. A queued question (§ 7) points at it across editions.
  • cash is required; tournament is present only when it differs. The lint test refuses a tournament whose note is empty and refuses a cash body over three paragraphs.
  • Every {{rule.key}} in a body must name a key in binds, and every key in binds must exist in TableRules (§ 5). The test resolves every body against a fully-set and a fully-unset TableRules and asserts both render.
  • status: 'draft' ships but does not show. It is how a situation gets into the file ahead of its answer being verified, so that search can capture interest in it (§ 7) without presenting it as ours.
  • The edition is one constant, RULES_EDITION = '2026.1', shown on the Rulings sheet's sub-line, on every situation sheet, and stamped into every queued question.

Why data in the bundle rather than fetched: the night is offline-first and a ruling is needed in the thirty seconds the argument lasts. An edition ships with an app release. Fetching a newer edition between releases is in § 12.

5 · The group's own rules

The research's third finding is that home games argue about money procedure and that no rulebook covers it: rebuy caps, going south, notice before leaving, who absorbs a short bank, rounding, IOUs. A 60-vote poll on the rebuy cap had no majority. So these cannot have one right answer; they are parameters the group sets, and the answer reads them.

5.1 TableRules, beside MoneyRule

MoneyRule is what comes off the table at settle-up — the bill, the piggy bank, a host fee — and ruleText.ts turns each one into a sentence. TableRules is how the table is run, and it is a different thing: one object per group, not a list, with no arithmetic of its own except where § 1 sends it to the engine.

interface TableRules {
  buyIn: { min: Money; max: Money } | null;       // the standard buy-in already in Settings is the default max
  shortBuy: 'one' | 'none' | 'any';               // Robert's: one short buy per session
  rebuyCap: { kind: 'none' } | { kind: 'largestStack'; percent: 50 | 75 | 100 } | { kind: 'fixed'; amount: Money } | null;
  goingSouth: 'forbidden' | 'allowed' | null;     // chips off the table mid-session
  leaveNotice: 'none' | 'oneOrbit' | 'thirtyMinutes' | 'oneHour' | null;
  returnWithin: 'oneHour' | 'never' | null;       // Robert's: back within an hour at the amount removed
  runItTwice: 'byAgreement' | 'never' | null;
  chops: 'allowed' | 'never' | null;
  straddle: 'none' | 'underTheGun' | 'button' | 'any' | null;
  button: 'dead' | 'moving' | null;
  rabbitHunt: 'allowed' | 'never' | null;
  checkRaise: 'allowed' | 'never' | null;
  shortBank: 'hostAbsorbs' | 'splitAll' | 'splitLosers' | 'decideThen' | null;
  credit: { kind: 'none' } | { kind: 'deadlineDays'; days: number } | null;
  decider: { kind: 'host' } | { kind: 'player'; playerId: PlayerId } | { kind: 'notInHand' } | null;
}

null means not set, and the answer says so. Rounding is not here: the night already carries a RoundingMode, and the rounding situation reads that.

5.2 The chain, and the snapshot

Table rules follow the money rules' chain exactly, because a host already understands it from club-rules.tsx's own comment:

this night (snapshot at open)  →  club default  →  not set
  • The club default is edited on the Table rules sheet from Settings → Rules.
  • A night takes a snapshot when it opens, next to its rules snapshot. A ruling read on the night cites the snapshot, not the current default, so a rule changed on Tuesday does not rewrite what Saturday's ruling said.
  • No mid-night edit, for the reason house-rules.tsx gives: changing a rule a night is running under is an act that needs a record, and until there is a record there is no edit. Over Tonight the Table rules sheet is read-only, with a line saying so (UNSURE — "Set before the night opens, in Settings").

5.3 How an answer reads them

A body says {{rule.rebuyCap}} and the renderer substitutes a sentence from a small map in core, the way rateLabel does for money rules:

Key Set Not set
rebuyCap Your group caps a rebuy at half the biggest stack. Your group has not set a cap. Most groups cap at half the biggest stack; some allow a full match.
shortBank Your group's rule: the host covers a short bank. Your group has not decided. The common rule is that whoever ran the bank covers it; some groups split it.
decider Lena decides disputes tonight. Nobody is named. Hosts usually decide; when the host is in the hand, pick someone who is not.

All UNSURE. The point is the shape: a set rule reads as the group's own, an unset one reads as ours and is honest about it, and the Your group row on the sheet gives the Change link either way.

5.4 Where the numbers go

Three situations compute rather than explain, and they go to packages/core:

  • sidePots(stacks, bets) — several players all in for different amounts; who is eligible for which pot. Pure, tested against the handoff's canonical night and against the textbook three-way example.
  • shortBank(rules, inPlay, accountedFor, players) — how much is missing and who carries it under the group's shortBank rule. Reads the same balance the count-up header reads; never its own sum.
  • rebuyAllowed(rules, stacks, player, amount) — yes or no, and the cap.

The AI (§ 6) is never asked these. If a question routes to one of them, the answer is the engine's figure with the engine's sentence under it.

6 · The AI fallback

6.1 When it runs

Only when search finds no verified situation, or when the host taps Ask under a result that did not fit. It never runs on its own, never pre-empts a verified answer, and never runs for a situation whose computes is set — those go to the engine, and a question the engine can answer is routed by search to the situation, not to the model.

6.2 Where it runs

On the server, never on the phone. A Supabase Edge Function, ruling-assist (name UNSURE), holds the API key, checks the caller's membership tier, rate limits per group, and writes the queue row (§ 7) in the same call. The phone sends:

  • the question as typed;
  • the game kind, always cash today;
  • the night's TableRules snapshot (or the club default when there is no night);
  • the ids of the three nearest verified situations, by search score, so the model answers in the library's own terms and the response can say closest verified situation: …;
  • the content edition.

Names are not sent as names. The phone replaces roster names in the question with Player A, Player B before sending — it knows the roster — and maps them back on display. The queue stores the anonymised text (§ 7).

6.3 The call

Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), one request, structured output, adaptive thinking at effort: 'medium'. Not an agent: there is no tool to call and no loop to run. The whole verified library is the system prompt, under a cache_control breakpoint with a one-hour TTL, so the per-question cost is the question and the answer, not the library. The server-side fallbacks: "default" parameter is on, so a refusal on the primary model is retried on a fallback inside the same call; a refusal that survives that is shown as Couldn't answer this one (UNSURE) and still queued.

The system prompt says, in substance:

  • You are answering for a home cash game. These are the verified situations; prefer their language. Cite the rule you rely on by name.
  • Do not do arithmetic. If the question needs a pot, a stack or a share counted, say which calculator answers it and stop.
  • Do not invent a house rule. If the answer depends on a parameter the group has not set, say the common default and that the group has not chosen.
  • If the ruling would differ in a tournament, say so in one sentence, separately.
  • If a fact is missing that changes the ruling (were the cards face up? had anyone acted?), ask for it instead of guessing.

The response shape, enforced by output_config.format:

interface AssistRuling {
  answer: string;                       // ≤ 3 paragraphs, the host's words
  reliesOn: Citation[];                 // what it cited
  tournamentDiffers: string | null;     // one sentence or null
  needsFact: string | null;             // a question back, or null
  nearestSituation: string | null;      // a verified id, or null
  confidence: 'high' | 'medium' | 'low';
}

needsFact renders as one question with a text field and a That's it button (UNSURE); the second call carries the first answer and the fact. Two rounds at most, then the answer stands as given.

6.4 How it is marked

The AI answer wears the situation sheet's layout with four differences, and all four are on the screen at once:

  1. The title is the host's question, and the sub-line reads AI answer · not verified · edition 2026.1 (UNSURE).
  2. A status pill after the title — the same Pill the app uses for Not settled — reading AI (UNSURE), in the amber tone with its 1px amber border: the token the app reserves for pending, unclaimed, unpaid, which is exactly what an unverified answer is. Louder than the muted tone, and still not a colour the app spends on anything but waiting.
  3. Source reads What the AI relied on (UNSURE) and lists reliesOn.
  4. The footer is Was this right? (UNSURE) 👍 👎, and under it one line: We'll check this one and add a verified answer (UNSURE).

A verified answer carries none of these; it carries the check and VERIFIED instead. The pill is the thing a host can see across the table.

6.5 Cost, so the tier decision has a number

Library in the system prompt ≈ 60 situations × 250 words ≈ 20k tokens, cached
Per question, uncached question + rules snapshot ≈ 600 tokens in, ≈ 400 out
Per question, at list price under one cent with the cache warm; a few cents cold

Which tiers get the AI is in § 12. The library itself should be free: the research names the printable rules sheet as the one acquisition hook in the category, and a library nobody can read captures no questions.

7 · The review queue

Every question that reaches Ask, every 👎, and every search that ended with no tap is a row we want. The loop is: capture → cluster → write → ship → the question stops arriving.

7.1 Capture

One row per event, written through the outbox like everything else the phone records, so a question asked with no signal arrives when the signal does:

create table ruling_question (
  id              uuid primary key,          -- client-generated: the idempotency key
  group_id        uuid not null references club(id),
  night_id        uuid references session(id),   -- null from Settings
  asked_at        timestamptz not null,
  kind            text not null check (kind in ('asked', 'thumbs_down', 'no_match')),
  question        text not null,             -- anonymised: Player A, Player B
  situation_id    text,                      -- the verified id a 👎 was on, or the nearest match
  answered_by     text not null check (answered_by in ('verified', 'ai', 'engine', 'none')),
  ai_answer       jsonb,                     -- the AssistRuling, when answered_by = 'ai'
  ai_model        text,                      -- the model id that answered
  rules_snapshot  jsonb not null,            -- the TableRules the answer was read against
  feedback        text check (feedback in ('helpful', 'wrong')),
  edition         text not null,
  app_version     text not null
);

Policies: the phone that wrote it may insert and may update feedback on its own rows; nobody on the client side may read another group's rows; reading everything is the service role, for us. The table names a group and, when there is one, a session, and it is not a ledger entry: verify.ts sums money, and a question is not money.

no_match rows are written locally on every search that ended without a tap and flushed in one batch when the sheet closes, so a host typing three variants of one phrase produces three rows with one asked_at minute, which is exactly the signal we want and easy to collapse.

7.2 Review

At first, no admin screen: a SQL view and an export.

create view ruling_question_review as
select lower(regexp_replace(question, '[^a-z ]', '', 'gi')) as stem,
       count(*)                       as asked,
       count(*) filter (where feedback = 'wrong') as marked_wrong,
       max(asked_at)                  as last_asked,
       mode() within group (order by situation_id) as nearest,
       array_agg(distinct edition)    as editions
from ruling_question
group by 1 order by asked desc;

npm run rulings:export (UNSURE — script name) pulls the view and the raw rows to a CSV. The workflow is: read the top of the view each week; for each stem with more than a handful of rows, write a situation, verify it, ship it in the next edition; the next release's no_match count for that stem goes to zero, which is the check that it worked. A 👎 on a verified answer is reviewed first, every time.

An admin page can come later and is not on the build order.

7.3 What we do not keep

The question as typed, with names. The phone anonymises before send (§ 6.2) and the row holds only that. A group name is not in the row either; group_id is. Nothing in the row identifies a person who is not the host, and the host is identified only as the writer of the night, which the server already knows.

8 · What is deliberately not recorded

The first draft of this file saved a ruling to the night: a night_ruling row beside night_pass, a Record this ruling step with Decided by and a note, a row in /log, a phrase in the settled night's meta, a line on the watcher's feed. The owner took it out on 22 October: the feature is for reference, and the night's record stays what it is today. Three reasons stand behind that, and they are worth keeping so the idea does not come back by accident:

  • The ledger is money. Every row the night carries is summed or verified by packages/core. A sentence about a mucked hand is neither, and the first non-money row invites the second.
  • A recorded decision is a thing people argue with later. The research's heated threads are all "was this right?" — a record with a name on it is a thing to re-litigate at cash-out, not a thing that ends the argument.
  • Nothing in the review loop needs it. The queue (§ 7) keeps the question, the rules it was read against and whether the answer helped. That is what we need to write the next edition. Who decided what, we do not need and should not hold.

What remains of the idea is the decider parameter in Table rules (§ 5), which answers who decides a dispute inside the answers, and nothing that writes.

9 · The situation list — first draft

Sixty situations, seven areas, in the order they should appear. ★ marks the twelve on the Rulings sheet. Every one-line answer is a DRAFT for the owner to verify before it is written up and marked verified; none of it is final copy. T says whether a tournament ruling differs. Binds names the group rule the answer reads, if any.

Betting

# What happened Cash answer (draft) T Binds
★ Said "raise", put in one chip Facing a bet, one big chip without saying "raise" is a call. Said "raise" first: it is a raise, and the amount is what they then put in. same —
★ Bet in two motions A bet goes in one motion or with the amount said first. Two trips to the stack without a number is a call of the first trip. same —
"I'll see your bet… and raise" The words "call" or "see" came first and they bind. It is a call. same —
★ Acted before their turn A check, call or fold out of turn stands if nobody before them changes the action. If somebody does, the early action is taken back and they may act again. same —
Verbal "all in", chips still in the stack Said in turn, it binds. The chips follow. same —
All-in for less than a full raise It does not reopen the betting for anyone who has already acted. They can call the extra, not re-raise. same —
Minimum raise At least the size of the last bet or raise in this round. same —
Check-raise Allowed unless your group has turned it off. same checkRaise
Straddle Whatever your group allows: none, under the gun, the button, or anyone. A straddle is a blind raise and acts last before the flop. differs — no straddles straddle
Betting out of turn on purpose Treat it as the out-of-turn rule, and tell them once. Twice is an angle (see Behaviour). same —

10 · Copy

Three rules for the words, beyond CLAUDE.md's:

  1. A title is what happened, in the words a player uses. "Winning hand hit the muck", not "Dead hand at showdown". Search reads aliases for the other phrasings.
  2. An answer is read aloud. Short sentences, no bullets, the ruling first and the reason second. The research's heated threads are all "was this right?", and a sentence the host can say is what ends them.
  3. The AI is named as AI every time it speaks. A pill, a sub-line, a changed footer. Never only one of them.

12 · Open

  1. Which tiers get the AI. The library free for all is argued above. The AI on Regular and Full, or Full only, is the owner's call and lands in membership.ts, the one seam, as a fifth function.
  2. The count-up hook. The short-bank row under the counted list is the one place this design touches a screen a cut owns. If the cut's author would not draw it, it goes, and the host reaches the situation from the drawer instead.
  3. Fetching an edition between releases. Data in the bundle is simplest and always works offline. A fetched edition would let a verified answer ship the week it is written. Worth doing once the queue shows questions arriving faster than releases.
  4. Robert's Rules citation style. Citing by section name is safe; quoting is not. Whether to cite at all, given the licence, is for the owner.

  6. Dealer-mucked hand and the money. § 9 leaves what happens to the mucked player's money as a group decision because the sources disagree. The owner may want a default.
  7. Two-player and short-handed blinds, dealer's choice games. Out of the first edition on purpose; the research shows them as lookups, not disputes.
