# Poker rulebooks, rulings columns, and search demand for rules questions

Researched 13 October 2026. Scope: rules, procedure and disputes (not strategy); English-language, US/UK. Two parts — A: the rulebooks and columns amateur players are pointed to, and what they themselves say recurs; B: search-demand evidence. Every figure is labelled with its source and date; third-party keyword-tool figures are estimates.

Method note on Part B: no keyword tool with a stated Google Keyword Planner / Ahrefs / Semrush provenance was reachable (TheSEOLabs returned HTTP 522, pokerseo.org HTTP 503, Google Trends is JavaScript-only and could not be fetched). The figures below come from (i) KeySearch's public "top poker keywords" page, (ii) RankHero keyword pages (an Etsy-oriented tool whose figures fall on Google Keyword Planner's bucket values — see Inferences), (iii) a TheSEOLabs figure captured in a search snippet, and (iv) Google's own autocomplete endpoint, which was fetched live on 13 Oct 2026 and is the strongest primary evidence here.

## Key question 1 — Which rulebook do amateur venues actually cite as the default, and how old / maintained is it?

### Takeaway
Two documents share the default: **Robert's Rules of Poker** (Bob Ciaffone) for cash/ring play and the **Poker TDA Rules** for tournaments. Robert's Rules is frozen — its author died in May 2022, his site is gone, and the text (Version 11 for cardrooms, a separate 62-page home-game edition) survives only on mirrors — while the TDA rules are actively maintained on a two-year cycle, with the 2026 edition current, but are tournament-only and never mention cash games.

### Cited Findings

**Robert's Rules of Poker (Bob Ciaffone)**
- Robert Ciaffone, 10 December 1940 – 13 May 2022, died at 81; longtime Card Player columnist; worked with the New Jersey Casino Control Commission on poker-room rules — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Ciaffone); [PokerNews obituary, May 2022](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2022/05/poker-columnist-bob-ciaffone-passes-away-at-age-81-41213.htm)
- The home-game edition's preamble: "This version of Robert's Rules of Poker is for private games … Ciaffone authored the rulebook for the Poker Players Association (founded in 1984, now defunct), the first comprehensive set of poker rules for the general public. He has done extensive work on rules for the Las Vegas Hilton, The Mirage, and Hollywood Park Casino" — [Robert's Rules of Poker, home-game edition, PDF mirrored at pagat.com](https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)
- The home edition is explicitly derived from the cardroom edition: "This rulebook for private games was made by taking the document constructed for cardroom use and making the appropriate changes … The restriction on the maximum number of raises on a betting round was set at a bet and three raises for all limit poker forms, which is the traditional rule for private games. The procedure for shuffling and cutting is described. The time one may be gone from a game has been shortened." It opens with a legal warning: "No state in our country allows a person to run a poker game as a business. Raking pots and charging an hourly rate for playing are two examples of activities only a licensed commercial cardroom would be allowed to do." — [same PDF](https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)
- Structure of the home edition (62 pages, 16 sections): Proper Behavior (conduct code, etiquette); House Policies (decision-making, procedures, seating); General Poker Rules (the buy-in, shuffle and cut, misdeals, dead hands, irregularities, betting and raising, the showdown, ties); Button and Blind Use; then game-by-game sections (Hold'em, Omaha, Omaha/8, Stud, Razz, Stud/8, Lowball, Draw, Kill Pots, No-limit and Pot-limit, Tournaments), an Explanations section and a glossary — [same PDF](https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)
- Distribution today: "his website was taken down" and the content "can still be found using the internet archive by finding the snapshots before 2020"; the thread links a November 2019 Wayback snapshot of the home-games version and a May 2019 snapshot of Version 11 — [PokerChipForum, "Cash Game Rulesets from the wayback machine", 3 Mar 2023](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/goto/post?id=2116556)
- Version 11 (the cardroom "standard") is also mirrored as a PDF at pagat.com — [pagat.com RobsPkrRules11.pdf](https://Pagat.Com/docs/RobsPkrRules11.pdf) — and by HomePokerTourney, which calls it "about as official as you get for poker" and distributes it as a 27-page full-size PDF and a booklet — [HomePokerTourney poker-rules page](https://www.homepokertourney.org/poker-rules.htm)
- Ciaffone's colleague Michael O'Malley, writing in Card Player, said Ciaffone "completed work on Robert's Rules of Poker, a comprehensive poker rulebook for cardrooms, and you can download a digital copy for free at his website" — [Card Player](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1036564) (undated column)
- How hobbyist hosts actually choose: "I am going to be Robert's for cash games and TDA for tournaments" (thread OP); "We use RRoP v11 for cash play" (reply, BGinGA) — [PokerChipForum, 3 Mar 2023](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/goto/post?id=2116556)
- Card Player's own "Rules of Poker" hub follows Robert's Rules' section headings verbatim — "The Buy-In," "Shuffling the Deck," "Misdeals," "Dead Hands," "Betting and Raising," "The Showdown," "Ties" — but names no author and does not attribute it to Ciaffone — [Card Player rules-of-poker](https://www.cardplayer.com/rules-of-poker)

**Poker TDA Rules**
- The Poker TDA is "a voluntary poker industry association founded in 2001"; its rules "are meant to supplement house rules, and in case of conflict with a gaming agency, the agency rules apply"; the association "meets once every two years" — [pokerfuse, "Everything you need to know about the TDA rule changes"](https://pokerfuse.com/features/in-depth/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-tda-rule-changes-20-08/)
- Current edition: "2026 TDA Poker Rules", distributed as Word (.docx) and PDF in a "Longform" (rules + Recommended Procedures + a "large illustration addendum") and a condensed "Short Form" (rules only, landscape, printable both sides), plus a "Longform Redlines" marking changes from the 2024 rules. Prior editions linked: 2024, 2022, 2019, 2017, 2015, 2013, 2011, 2009. Summits: 2026 (XII), 2024 (XI), 2022 (X), 2019 (IX), 2017 (VIII), 2015 (VII), 2013 (VI), 2011 (V), 2009 (IV), 2007 (III). Foreign-language translations are linked through the forum; a "Poker TDA Mobile App" page exists. **The rules page does not mention cash games and has no FAQ section — rule questions are directed to the Poker TDA Discussion Forum** — [pokertda.com/poker-tda-rules](https://www.pokertda.com/poker-tda-rules/)
- Forum size (footer, fetched 13 Oct 2026): 2,032 members, 13,929 posts, 1,687 topics. Board sizes: "Rules & Procedures Questions, General" 9,649 posts / 922 topics; "Live Cash Game Rules Questions" 936 posts / 114 topics; "Non-TDA Tournament and General Poker Rules" 425 / 57; "Official TDA Rules (latest)" 162 / 19; "Recommended Procedures (latest)" 147 / 21; "Mobile App" 46 / 14; foreign-language translations 47 / 13 — [pokertda.com/forum](https://www.pokertda.com/forum/)
- HomePokerTourney distributes the TDA rules too, but its hosted copy is the **2013** rulebook (13-page full-size, 3 double-sided-page booklet; Chinese, French, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish and Russian versions), and the page is copyright 2017 — [HomePokerTourney poker-rules page](https://www.homepokertourney.org/poker-rules.htm)

**HomePokerTourney (HPT) rulebook**
- "HPT Poker Tournament Rule Book, Version 11" combines HPT tournament rules, "Robert's Rules of Poker (Condensed Hold'em version)", how to play and deal, and charts; 19-page full-size PDF and 7 double-sided-page booklet; also .doc. The site also lists WSOP official tournament rules 2011–2015 and WSOP dealer/clerk/live-action handbooks (only 2011 files directly linked). The rules page covers tournaments only — "Poker ring games" exists in the menu but there are no cash-game rules on the page — [HomePokerTourney poker-rules page](https://www.homepokertourney.org/poker-rules.htm)
- HPT's own NLHE rulebook: "These HomePokerTourney No-limit Texas Hold'em poker tournament rules are to be used in conjunction with 'Robert's Rules of Poker'", with HPT's rules winning in conflicts — [HPT poker-rulebook-tourney.pdf](https://www.homepokertourney.org/docs/rulebook/poker-rulebook-tourney.pdf)

**Cooke's Rules of Real Poker**
- Roy Cooke and John Bond, ConJelCo, 2005, 192 pages, listed at $9.95; aims to standardise rules for games spread in public cardrooms; "the rulebooks of nearly every major cardroom in the country were consulted"; each rule has a preferred version with alternates; where one rule would limit action and another induce it, the authors prefer the action-inducing one — [Wordup Books listing](https://wordupbooks.com/book/9781886070226); [Wikipedia: Roy Cooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Cooke)
- Hobbyist view of it: "not freely available but a rather inexpensive e-book download"; the only free copy is a 2003 Wayback snapshot of Poker1 — [PokerChipForum, 3 Mar 2023](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/goto/post?id=2116556)

**WSOP rules**
- WSOP publishes a yearly tournament rulebook; PokerNews' May 2025 summary says "most of the rules remain consistent with past editions", with 2025 changes to the late-arrival policy (Rule 16), player bans (Rule 18) and electronic devices banned "from the final three tables onward" — [PokerNews, May 2025](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2025/05/electronic-devices-dodging-the-blinds-and-more-6-wsop-rule-c-48524.htm); [pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/live-and-online/222122-phones-electronic-devices-banned-during-WSOP-2025/)
- 2026 WSOP rule changes are reported separately — [vip-grinders](https://www.vip-grinders.com/news/wsop-2026-rule-changes/)
- The official PDF URL could not be located (wsop.com/2025/rules/ returned 404 on 13 Oct 2026); treat WSOP rules as tournament-only. HomePokerTourney's mirrored copies stop at 2015 — [HomePokerTourney](https://www.homepokertourney.org/poker-rules.htm)

### Inferences
- For an amateur **cash** game there is effectively one named authority, Robert's Rules, and it is unmaintained since 2022 with no official home; the TDA is the only maintained body and it is tournament-only. That is a gap a cash-game product can legitimately fill by restating (not inventing) the Robert's Rules cash provisions.
- The 2013 TDA copy on HomePokerTourney — the site Google autocomplete and forum posters point hobbyists to — is 13 years and six editions behind the 2026 TDA rules. Amateur venues citing "TDA rules" are often citing a stale copy.

### Gaps
- No survey or census of which rulebook home games or small cardrooms adopt; the "default" above is inferred from forum practice, HPT's bundling, and Card Player's unattributed reuse of Robert's Rules' structure.
- The WSOP 2025/2026 rulebook PDF itself was not retrieved, so the rule count and any live-action (cash) provisions are unverified.

## Key question 2 — What do the rulebooks and columns say are the most common floor calls or disputes?

### Takeaway
Across the sources, the recurring disputes are a short, stable list: the **one-chip rule / single chip call-or-raise**, **string bets and forward motion**, **verbal declarations ("all in", calling before the amount)**, **showdown order (who shows first)**, **exposed or mis-dealt cards and dealer errors**, **button/blind placement (dead button, missed blinds, heads-up SB)**, and in cash games specifically **running it twice, straddles, and whether a player must pay after a dealer error**. Only one source (Matt Savage) quantifies anything — "the one chip rule" is the most-asked question — and only the TDA forum view-counts give a ranked signal.

### Cited Findings

**Named "most common" items in the sources**
- Matt Savage (WPT executive tour director, TDA co-founder): "The most commonly asked question is the one chip rule (whereby if only one chip is thrown over the line it is classed as a call, not a raise, unless declared verbally)"; new players raise it most. Volume of rules questions: "in the neighbourhood of 25–30 a day" in summer, "maybe 5–10" on other days, considerably more during the WSOP — [PokerStrategy, "I Get 30 Ruling Tweets A Day" (page updated 22 Feb 2026; original interview undated, older)](https://www.pokerstrategy.com/?p=45939)
- TDA 2015 revision: "One of the most common questions asked of tournament directors has been, 'What is rabbit hunting?' Therefore, this rule was cleared up by adding the definition"; same cut moved the seat-requirement to "at the time the very first card is dealt" — [pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/features/in-depth/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-tda-rule-changes-20-08/)
- TDA showdown ruling, Card Player report on new TDA rules: "Many of the tournament directors in attendance formerly made the last aggressive action show first, even if the last round of betting was checked" — the disagreement among directors themselves is why the rule was written down — [Card Player, "New rules adopted by the TDA"](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65666-paul-wasicka-20-9/articles/16728-new-rules-adopted-by-the-tournament-directors-association)
- Card Player columnist Michael O'Malley posed "four scenarios containing what I thought were difficult decisions" and "received more than 200 e-mails with decisions"; reader letters he answered include a player who looked at another's hand before action — [Card Player](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1029069); [Card Player](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1027154)
- Other Card Player rulings columnists surfaced: Jan Fisher; Jack McClelland ("Ask Jack"); an unnamed "Rules Guy"; a Jamie Gold column arguing "cash games generally rule that once there is action, a hand counts"; a column on hands read by another player before tabling ("if the hand is read by another player before being tabled, it's dead") — [Card Player](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1033555); [Card Player](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1028669)
- Card Player's "Common Poker Situations" Q&A (the only FAQ-style block in any rules hub found): "What happens if someone acts out of turn?", "What if everyone folds?", "What if a card is exposed?", "What if two players tie?"; it also gives cash-game minimum buy-ins of "roughly 30–50 big blinds" — [Card Player rules-of-poker](https://www.cardplayer.com/rules-of-poker)
- Glossary framing of a "ruling": requested "when there's ambiguity or disagreement about proper procedure, such as a string bet, an exposed card, an angle shoot, or a misdeal" — [DeucesCracked glossary: ruling](https://www.deucescracked.com/learn/poker-glossary/ruling); floor staff "resolve disputes between players such as string bets or angle shooting allegations" — [DeucesCracked glossary: floor](https://www.deucescracked.com/learn/poker-glossary/floor)
- Home-game guides converge on one procedural rule rather than a list: "every home game needs someone who has the final say in disagreements" — [Advanced Poker Training, hosting a home game](https://advancedpokertraining.com/poker/articles/beyond-basics/hosting-a-home-game.php); HPT: the TD should have final say; the right to dispute a finished hand ends when the next hand begins — [HPT rulebook PDF](https://www.homepokertourney.org/docs/rulebook/poker-rulebook-tourney.pdf)

**What Robert's Rules (home edition) singles out for explanation or prohibition — i.e. where the author expected argument**
- House policies: "All games are table stakes … The amount bought must be announced to the table, or only the amount of the minimum buy-in plays"; "If you return to the game within one hour of cashing out, your buy-in must be equal to the amount removed"; "Splitting pots by agreement will not be allowed. Chopping the big and small blind … may be allowed in non-tournament button games, if that is customary"; "Insurance propositions are not allowed. Dealing twice (or three times) when all-in is permitted at big-bet poker"; "No one is allowed to play another player's chips"; "Pushing bets ('saving' or 'potting out') is not allowed" — [Robert's Rules home edition PDF](https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)
- Buy-in: "A full buy-in at pot-limit or no-limit poker is forty times the minimum bring-in (usually, the size of the big blind)"; "Only one short buy-in is allowed per session"; "Adding to your stack is not considered a buy-in, and may be done in any quantity between hands" — [same](https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)
- Showdown order: "If everyone checks (or is all-in) on the final betting round, the player who acted first is the first to show the hand. If there is wagering on the final betting round, the last player to take aggressive action by a bet or raise is the first to show … If there is a side pot, players involved in the side pot should show their hands before anyone who is all-in for only the main pot"; "Cards speak … deliberately miscalling a hand with the intent of causing another player to discard a winning hand is unethical and may result in forfeiture of the pot"; a player other than the winner who asks to see a folded hand makes that hand dead — [same](https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)
- Etiquette list opens with the things that cause arguments: "Deliberately acting out of turn. Deliberately splashing chips into the pot. Agreeing to check a hand out when a third player is all-in. Reading a hand for another player at the showdown before it has been placed faceup on the table. Telling anyone to turn a hand faceup at the showdown." — [same](https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)
- The Explanations section's ten items are the author's list of contested rules: moving vs dead button; the no-burn redeal on a premature flop ("misguided"); new players entering between the blinds; dead hands with the wrong number of cards at stud; "This rulebook requires all cash to be changed into chips … If the game chooses to allow cash, only large bills should be permitted"; the raise cap (a bet and three raises "cuts down on the effect of collusion"); absent players' hands in tournaments — [same](https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)

**The rules "explainer" series (PokerNews, Upswing) — what they do not cover**
- PokerNews' poker-rules hub is a beginner's guide: Hold'em/Omaha/Stud plus ~20 variants, hand rankings, blinds, limit vs no-limit, "The Table Stakes". No cash-game section, no home-game section, no etiquette section, and "String bets, misdeals, showdown order, and side pots are not covered"; no FAQ — [PokerNews poker-rules](https://www.pokernews.com/poker-rules/)
- PokerNews does cover disputes in standalone strategy pieces: "How to Handle Disputes When Playing Poker in a Casino" — [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/how-to-handle-disputes-when-playing-poker-in-a-casino-20435.htm); "The Showdown: Rules, Procedures, and Etiquette" — [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/the-showdown-rules-procedures-and-etiquette-19237.htm); and the cash-specific "Casino Poker for Beginners" series (table stakes, "going south … 'squirrelling' or 'ratholing' … considered highly unethical") — [PokerNews, "Talking Table Stakes"](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/casino-poker-for-beginners-018-talking-table-stakes-29976.htm); [PokerNews, "No, You Can't Bet Your Covered Wagon"](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/casino-poker-for-beginners-table-stakes-covered-wagon-22065.htm)
- Upswing's "How to Play Poker: Basic Poker Rules for New Players": hand rankings, blinds/antes, limit vs no-limit, table stakes (standard NL buy-in 100 BB), Hold'em and Omaha basics; home games, etiquette, string bets, misdeals and side pots not covered; no FAQ; no author or date (© 2026) — [Upswing poker-rules](https://upswingpoker.com/poker-rules/)
- "Going south" rule summary: "normally prohibited for a player to remove a portion of his or her chips from the table" — [Wikipedia: Cash game](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_game); the legal alternative is to cash out entirely and wait ("recycling … I've never seen it lower than 30 minutes"); in home games "you will see this rule broken a lot", and a common fix is "to have one person handle all chip transactions" — [PokerListings, odd rules and exceptions](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-guides/odd-poker-rules-and-exceptions)

### Inferences
- The disputes that rulebooks address are almost all *in-hand procedure*. The money disputes that break a home cash game — the cash-out not reconciling, unrecorded rebuys, who owes whom — appear in none of the rulebooks beyond Robert's Rules' three house-policy lines (table stakes announced to the table, return within an hour at the same amount, cash changed to chips). They appear instead in hobbyist forums and app-vendor blogs (see KQ5).
- The TDA cash-game board's top threads (dealer error payment, running it twice, straddles, moving vs dead button, single-chip) are a direct, if small-sample, ranking of what cardroom staff themselves find hard in cash games.

### Gaps
- No source publishes a frequency count of floor calls by type. "Most common" rests on one Savage quote, one TDA note about rabbit hunting, forum view-counts and recurrence across columns.
- Card Player's rules columns were sampled through search snippets; the full archive by Ciaffone, O'Malley, Fisher and McClelland was not read, and the Linda Johnson "ask the dealer"-type column named in the brief was not found at all.

## Key question 3 — Which rules queries have the largest search volume, and which rise seasonally?

### Takeaway
Hand-ranking queries dwarf procedure queries by an order of magnitude: estimates put "poker hands" near 1.0–1.5M searches/month and "poker rules" at roughly 135–200k, against "poker hand rankings" at ~75–110k; individual procedure questions ("string bet", "side pot", "who shows first") do not register in any public volume list found. The only month-by-month series obtained (RankHero, Sep 2025–Aug 2026) peaks in December–January, not June — a holiday/home-game pattern — and the WSOP-season signal found was for rules *questions to directors* (Savage) and generic "poker" interest, not for rules keywords. No Google Trends series could be retrieved; this is the main gap.

### Inferences
- RankHero's monthly values (135,000 / 165,000 / 201,000; 74,000 / 90,500 / 110,000; 1,000,000 / 1,220,000 / 1,500,000) are exactly Google Keyword Planner's bucket values, so these are very likely rounded GKP global figures relabelled — reasonable as order-of-magnitude estimates, useless for fine ranking.
- "poker hands" at ~1M+ is almost certainly mixed intent (hand-history videos, "poker hands" apps, famous hands) and should not be read as pure rules demand; "poker hand rankings" (~90k) and "poker rules" (~165k) are the cleaner rules-intent figures.
- Ranking by what the evidence supports: (1) hand-ranking questions ("poker hands", "poker hand rankings", the "does a flush beat…" family) ≫ (2) "poker rules" / "texas holdem rules" / "how to play" ≫ (3) individual procedure questions (all-in, split pot, kicker, check-raise, who shows first, misdeal, string bet, side pot), which exist in autocomplete but appear in no published volume list.
- Seasonality is December-led for rules keywords (holiday home games), with a weaker, unquantified summer/WSOP effect; this is the opposite of the brief's assumption that June/July leads.

### Gaps
- No Google Trends comparison could be run (JS-only). The report writer should treat the December-peak finding as single-tool evidence.
- No Ahrefs/Semrush figure for any rules query; no US-only or UK-only volume for anything except RankHero's "poker rules" US share (40,500, 25%).
- No volumes at all for "string bet", "side pot calculation", "who shows first at showdown", "poker misdeal rules", "can you check raise", "split pot poker", "poker kicker rules", "texas holdem rules for beginners", "does a flush beat a straight".

## Key question 5 — Is there evidence of demand for cash-game-specific rules (table stakes, buy-in minimums, "going south", rebuys, chopping, settling up / bank) as opposed to generic hand-ranking questions?

### Takeaway
Yes, but it is small and it lives in different places. Google autocomplete for "poker cash game rules" is entirely cash-specific — wsop cash game rules (and pdf), **home poker cash game rules**, **poker cash game house rules**, **poker cash game buy in rules**, "are cash poker games illegal" — and "home poker games" surfaces legality, chip setup and starting chips. On the TDA forum, cash-game questions are about 10% of rules traffic (936 of ~11,000 question posts). Settling-up and bank-reconciliation questions are absent from every rulebook and column, and appear only in hobbyist forums and app-vendor content — which is itself evidence of unmet demand, though no volume figure exists.

### Inferences
- Cash-game rules demand splits into (a) *procedure* — straddles, running it twice, button placement, one-chip — which the TDA forum and Robert's Rules do answer, and (b) *money handling* — buy-in minimums, rebuys, going south, chopping, bank reconciliation, who owes whom — which only Robert's Rules touches, in a few lines, and which hosts are currently answering for themselves on forums or via settlement apps.
- Autocomplete's "poker cash game house rules" and "home poker cash game rules" are the nearest thing to a direct demand signal for a house-rules sheet for a home cash game; its co-occurrence with "are cash poker games illegal" and "home poker games legal" means any such sheet will be read alongside a legality question (Robert's Rules opens with exactly that warning).
- Relative to hand-ranking demand (hundreds of thousands to over a million searches a month), cash-game rules demand is at least one to two orders of magnitude smaller — a niche with no authoritative, maintained source rather than a mass-market query.

### Gaps
- No search-volume figure for any cash-game-specific phrase ("poker cash game rules", "going south poker", "poker rebuy rules", "chop the pot", "poker settle up"); autocomplete presence is the only quantitative-ish signal.
- No UK-specific evidence of any kind for cash-game rules demand.
- Reddit (r/poker, r/homepoker) was not sampled; it would likely be the richest free source of settling-up and house-rules questions.
