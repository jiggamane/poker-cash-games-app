# Competitive scan: poker rules / ruling apps and the rules features inside home-game apps (as of 13 October 2026)

Scope note: this scan covers (A) standalone poker rules, hand-ranking and ruling apps on the Apple App Store, Google Play and the web, and (B) home-game management, ledger and tournament-timer apps, looking specifically for a rulebook, an "ask a ruling" feature, a house-rules editor, a dispute log or an FAQ. Strategy trainers, solvers and online poker clients are excluded unless they contain a rules feature. Numbers are quoted from the page they were read on, with the date of reading (all read 13 October 2026 unless stated); App Store pages often omit the year on a version date, which is noted where it applies. Reddit could not be fetched from this environment, so community sentiment comes from forum threads and app-store reviews instead.

## Key question 1: Which poker rules apps exist, how popular are they, are they maintained, and are they mostly hand-ranking cheat sheets?

### Takeaway
The standalone "rules" category is tiny and almost entirely hand-ranking cheat sheets and beginner tutorials with single-digit to low-double-digit rating counts; the only serious rules products are two free TDA-affiliated iPad apps (a rulebook reader and a plain-language Q&A assistant) aimed at tournament floor staff, and neither has enough App Store ratings to display a score. Nothing found is built for cash-game rulings or for home games.

### Cited Findings

**Rulebook / ruling apps (the only true "rules" products found)**

- *Official Poker TDA Rules* (iOS, seller Stewart Bishop, copyright Poker Tournament Directors Association): free, no IAP, Education category, "designed for iPad, not verified for macOS"; contains the complete TDA rulebook with keyword/category/full-list search, light/dark mode, and links to the TDA site, forum, membership, certification and Summit; latest version 3.6 dated 12/11/2024 (3.5 on 12/09/2024, 3.4 on 12/05/2024, all "slight grammatical updates" and removed photos); the page shows "not enough ratings to display an overview"; developer states no data collected — [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6738895825)
- The same app appears twice in a Google Play search for "poker rules", listed under developer "The Poker TDA", with one listing showing a 4.5 rating (no rating count or install count visible in the fetched page) — [Google Play search "poker rules"](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=poker%20rules&c=apps)
- The TDA's own site links a "Poker TDA App for Android & Apple" and a "Poker TDA Mobile App" page, and a news item "New Poker TDA Mobile App Just Released" dated 07/13 (year not shown); the site also states Summit XII drew "176 members from 41 countries" and that the current rules are the **2026** Poker TDA Rules (3 new rules, 13 edits, 6 new Recommended Procedures approved at Summit XII) — [pokertda.com](https://www.pokertda.com/)
- A TDA page (last substantive text referencing Summit IX, 2019) says only that the mobile apps "put the TDA Poker Tournament Rules in the palm of your hand"; no platform links, release dates or download figures are given — [pokertda.com/?p=45](https://pokertda.com/?p=45)
- *Poker TD Assistant* (iOS, developer FEDERACAO MUNDIAL DE POQUER / World Poker Federation, "developed with the Tournament Directors Association"): free, no IAP listed, Reference category, designed for iPad, 11.7 MB; users "ask ruling questions in plain language", with voice input, in-app rulebook browsing and a ruling history; every answer "references the official TDA **2024** Rules"; languages English, Portuguese, Spanish, with French and Chinese added in v1.1.0; version 1.0 dated Jun 22 and v1.1.0 dated Jul 14 (year not shown, copyright 2026); the developer advises that "critical rulings should be verified against the official rulebook"; the page shows no ratings or reviews; privacy section says search history, user content and device identifiers are "linked to you" — [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6776635266)
- A search for AI "floor decision" assistants returned Poker TD Assistant as the only poker-specific tool; the nearest analogues are board-game referee bots (Ludus Judge AI, which "cites the exact page of an uploaded rulebook" and "was built because generic LLMs can invent rules") and a Wizards of the Coast patent for a confidence-gated Magic: The Gathering rules assistant — [WebSearch results, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.producthunt.com/products/ludus-judge-ai); [Draftsim on the WotC AI assistant](https://draftsim.com/mtg-ai-assistant-wotc/)
- The International Poker Rules Association (Marcel Luske) launched a rules site and mobile app in May 2013; no current store listing surfaced for it in this scan — [PokerNews, May 2013](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2013/05/marcel-luske-s-international-poker-rules-association-15045.htm)

### Inferences
- Demand for a "rules app" as such is thin on the stores: the best-maintained rules product (Poker TD Assistant) has zero displayed ratings months after launch, and the cheat sheets have 3–22 ratings each. The store category is effectively unoccupied rather than crowded.
- Both TDA products are tournament-only by construction (the TDA rulebook is tournament rules) and are iPad-first. Nobody found addresses cash-game procedure (table stakes, going south, rebuy rules, time-outs, splitting pots on request) or home-game house rules.
- Maintenance is a visible weakness even at the top: Poker TD Assistant answers from the TDA 2024 rules while the TDA has published 2026 rules; the TDA rulebook reader's last update was December 2024 and consisted of grammar fixes.
- The two review complaints that exist for a rules-adjacent app (Poker Hands Quiz) are both "no explanations": people want the reasoning, not just the answer.

### Gaps
- No download or install estimates were obtainable for any standalone rules app: chrome-stats.com returned 403 for the TDA app, no Google Play detail pages surfaced for TDA Rules, Maos do Poker or Poker Skill, and Sensor Tower/AppFollow pages for these apps did not appear in search.
- Playtika's *How to Poker* rating count and last update could not be read (404 on US storefront).
- Whether the International Poker Rules Association app (2013) still exists was not verified.
- No Android "poker rules" app with a large rating count was found; the search tool could not confirm whether such an app exists or simply did not surface.

## Key question 2: Do home-game apps already embed a rulebook or a dispute/ruling mechanic, and what do their users ask for?

### Takeaway
None of the home-game apps examined — PokerNow, Pokerrrr 2, the new wave of iOS buy-in/settlement trackers (ChipUp, Tilt, PokerSquad, HG Poker, Pokertally, StackPro, PartyPot), or the tournament clocks — includes a rulebook, a ruling tool, a house-rules editor, a dispute log or a rules FAQ. "Rules" in these products means game-variant and table-option toggles (straddle, run it twice, bomb pot), and user reviews ask for trust, stability and money-handling features, not rules content.

**What home-game players actually do when a ruling is needed**

- In a PokerChipForum "home game ruling" thread about a single oversized chip thrown in without saying "raise" in a 1-2-5 PLO game, most posters ruled it a call; the hosts were both in the hand and disagreed, so the raise stood; references cited were Robert's Rules of Poker v11, the TDA rules, a custom rulebook "following RRoP and TDA, reorganized for searchability", pokercoach.us, and a forum thread of hosting rules; one poster advised asking a nearby casino for its rulebook. **No app or website for settling rulings was mentioned by anyone.** — [PokerChipForum thread](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/threads/home-game-ruling.42199/latest)
- PokerNews guidance: house rules cover betting limits, variants, misdeals and disputes; "a neutral party, often the host, may be named the final authority"; home rules "tend to be looser than casino rules" and depend on everyone agreeing before the game — [PokerNews: House Rules](https://www.pokernews.com/pokerterms/house-rules.htm); [PokerNews: disputes in a casino](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/how-to-handle-disputes-when-playing-poker-in-a-casino-20435.htm)

### Inferences
- The home-game tooling market split in 2025–2026 into (a) online play-money clients with huge audiences (Pokerrrr 2 ~2.3M installs) and (b) a crowd of near-identical iOS settle-up trackers with 0–7 ratings each, all monetising with $2–5/month subscriptions or per-game tickets. Rules content is absent from both halves; the only "rules" vocabulary is variant toggles.
- The one place users do raise rule-adjacent concerns is trust in the host/app (Pokerrrr 2's "favours the host" reviews, "people not trusting the app"). A transparent ruling record is adjacent to that complaint but nobody has shipped one.
- Home hosts who care about rulings carry a rulebook (RRoP, TDA) or a self-made searchable one; the demand is real but currently satisfied by PDFs and forum posts, i.e. no product has made the carrying convenient.
- A ledger app already has the two inputs a ruling tool needs — the seated players and the stakes — which no standalone rules app has.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/homepoker, r/poker) could not be fetched; the search tool returned no Reddit threads for any query. Community wish-lists for PokerNow/Pokerrrr 2 are therefore inferred from App Store reviews and one Indie Hackers thread.
- PokerNow Plus pricing and user counts were not on the page; Poker Mavens and PPPoker were unreachable or unsearchable.
- Google Play ratings/installs for the settle-up apps were not found (most are iOS-only).
- No support-forum content for ChipUp/Tilt/PokerSquad exists yet (apps are months old).

## Key question 3: Is there any app or site that lets a player describe a table situation and get a ruling (decision tree or AI), and how good are reviews?

### Takeaway
Exactly one such product exists — Poker TD Assistant (World Poker Federation + TDA), an iPad app that answers plain-language ruling questions from the TDA 2024 tournament rules — and it has no displayed ratings or reviews; beyond it there are only generic ChatGPT "poker" GPTs (strategy-oriented, Plus-only) and an AI "coach" app misleadingly named PokerJudge. No decision-tree or interactive walkthrough tool was found anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Poker TD Assistant: "ask ruling questions in plain language", voice input, ruling history, answers "reference the official TDA 2024 Rules", aimed at "Tournament Directors, Floor Persons, and Dealers"; the developer says "critical rulings should be verified against the official rulebook"; no ratings or reviews shown; v1.1.0 added a notification centre and "a feedback area for suggestions and rating answers" — [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6776635266)
- *PokerJudge* (iOS, Product Hunt launch, hunter "Epic Chain"): tagline "AI poker coach, bankroll tracking, and tools—in your pocket"; "talk or type through a hand for AI coaching", cash/MTT session tracking; 0 upvotes, 1 comment, ranked 356th on its launch day, not featured; the page does not describe rulings — [hunted.space mirror of Product Hunt](https://hunted.space/product/pokerjudge)
- ChatGPT GPTs: *Poker GPT* (Michael Gredenberg) is "a friendly and casual Texas Hold'em poker companion… not intended as a professional poker training tool"; *Poker Pal* "covers a broad spectrum of topics, from clarifying basic game rules to offering strategic play suggestions" and "requires a ChatGPT Plus subscription"; *Poker Hand Analyzer* runs Monte Carlo simulations; an OpenAI community reply on a user-built Poker GPT: "No it is not good." — [genai.works Poker GPT](https://genai.works/gpts/poker-gpt); [robopost Poker Pal](https://robopost.app/en/toolpasta/tools/poker-pal); [OpenAI community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/paper-are-chatgpt-and-gpt-4-good-poker-players/583459)
- Academic evidence on LLM poker competence is strategy-only (pre-flop play), not rulings: "Both ChatGPT and GPT-4 are NOT game theory optimal poker players" — [arXiv 2308.12466v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12466v2)
- Outside poker, Ludus Judge AI (board games) positions itself on citing "the exact page of an uploaded rulebook" because "generic LLMs can invent rules"; a WotC patent describes an MTG rules assistant that scores confidence and either rules or asks for more detail — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/ludus-judge-ai); [Draftsim, Apr 2026](https://draftsim.com/mtg-ai-assistant-wotc/)
- Reference texts the "ask a ruling" workflow currently relies on: Robert's Rules of Poker v11 and a home-game edition are hosted free as PDFs on pagat.com; the TDA rules are "available in downloadable and printable PDF and Word formats"; the TDA also runs a discussion forum (activity not measurable from the fetched page) — [pagat.com RRoP v11](https://Pagat.Com/docs/RobsPkrRules11.pdf); [pagat.com RRoP home edition](https://www.Pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf); [pokertda.com](https://www.pokertda.com/)

### Inferences
- The "describe a situation, get a ruling" space has one entrant, it is institutional (WPF/TDA), tournament-only, iPad-first, and already a rules version behind. There is no consumer, cash-game, home-game or Android product doing this.
- The GPT-store listings are strategy chatbots wearing a "rules" word in the description; none is grounded in a rulebook or cites rule numbers, and Plus-only access blocks the casual home player.
- The adjacent board-game referee products show the design pattern that reviewers reward: cite the rule text, express uncertainty, ask for the missing fact.

### Gaps
- No independent review, accuracy test or user commentary on Poker TD Assistant was found (no ratings, no press coverage surfaced).
- GPT-store conversation counts for the poker GPTs were not visible in the directories reached; chatgpt.com itself did not appear in search results.
- No Poe or Character.ai poker-rules bot surfaced in search; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- No GitHub repository or indie-hacker post for a poker ruling decision tree surfaced (searches returned PartyPot marketing and strategy solvers instead).

## Key question 4: What do users complain about in reviews of existing rules apps?

### Takeaway
There are too few reviews of rules apps to form a statistical picture; the complaints that do exist are "no explanations" (Poker Hands Quiz), crashes (ChipUp), freezing and distrust of fairness (Pokerrrr 2), and ads (WSOP-style social apps). No review found complains about outdated rules or missing cash-game rules, because no app offers enough rules content for that complaint to arise.

### Cited Findings
- Poker Hands Quiz (3.4/5, 22 ratings): "impossibly hard quiz with no explanations"; "Developer hastily wrote this app with no help system and no explanations"; "Once I answer a quiz on how many outs, it shows the answer but removes the original cards" — [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1547131406)
- ChipUp (1.0/5, 2 ratings): "As soon as I open up a group, the app crashes" (Jan 25) — [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chipup/id6747723921)
- Pokerrrr 2: "The app freezes lots… if you exit to view other apps or text messages the app freezes and locks up"; allegations of host advantage; a club owner requesting pre-funded deposits — [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id592081067)
- Both TDA apps and Tilt show "hasn't received enough ratings or reviews to display an overview" — [Apple App Store TDA Rules](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6738895825); [Apple App Store TD Assistant](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6776635266); [Apple App Store Tilt](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6761335586)
- Poker Hands - Cheat Sheet's two reviews praise simplicity ("Simple, yet exactly what I was looking for!") and the listing's selling points are "no in-app purchases and no ads" — [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6757372869)
- Poker Hands Quiz and Poker Hands - Cheat Sheet both advertise offline use and no ads as features — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1547131406); [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6757372869)

### Inferences
- Review volume on rules apps is so low that "what users complain about" is better read from what the listings defensively promise: offline, no ads, no subscription, explanations. Those are the expectations.
- The one substantive content complaint — answer without explanation — maps directly onto a ruling tool: a verdict with no cited rule and no reasoning will be rejected at a table.

### Gaps
- Google Play review text for any rules app was not retrievable.
- No aggregator (AppFollow, Sensor Tower) review-sentiment page for these apps surfaced.
- A targeted search for "outdated / no cash-game rules / no house-rule customisation" complaints returned only spam wiki pages about the WSOP app's ads; no genuine review with those complaints was found.

## Key question 5: Is there evidence of willingness to pay for rules content?

### Takeaway
Weak and indirect. Paid rules-adjacent apps exist at $0.99–$1.99 (Poker Hands Quiz, Learn Poker) with tiny rating counts; the canonical rulebook (Robert's Rules of Poker) was withdrawn from sale and is now free, and the TDA apps are free; the money in home-game apps is being charged for game management ($3.99–$4.99/month or per-game tickets), not for rules. Amazon sales ranks and review counts for poker rule books could not be retrieved.

## Key question 6: Are there precedents of a settle-up or ledger app adding a rules or etiquette feature?

### Takeaway
No precedent was found: no poker ledger, settle-up, chip-count or tournament-clock app in this scan carries a rulebook, etiquette guide, house-rules editor or dispute log, and PartyPot's own marketing explicitly frames the gap in Splitwise-style apps as money mechanics (buy-in, rebuy, side pot), not rules.

### Cited Findings
- The 2025–2026 cohort of settle-up apps (ChipUp, Tilt, PokerSquad, HG Poker, Pokertally, StackPro, Home Poker - Track Buy-Ins, SettleChip, Poker Buy Manager, Upstack, PartyPot) list buy-in/rebuy/cash-out tracking, minimum-transfer settlement, leaderboards, clubs, roles/permissions and payment-handle storage; none lists a rules or etiquette feature — see listings cited under Key question 2.
- ChipUp's description says its records "help avoid arguments over missing money" — the only dispute-adjacent claim found, and it is about the ledger, not rulings — [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chipup/id6747723921)
- StackPro: "only the host can make changes during a session" and a "balance checker confirms chip totals match" — host-authority and audit mechanics, no rules — [Apple App Store (HK)](https://apps.apple.com/hk/app/stackpro/id6745120644)
- PartyPot on Splitwise: "built for household bills, rent, and shared trips and has no concept of a live buy-in, a re-buy, a side pot, or a pot split" — [PartyPot FAQ](https://partypotapp.com.advisoryapps.com/faq/best-app-settle-poker-debts-friends/)
- Tournament clocks (Texas Holdem Poker Timer Pro, BlindClock, Poker Timer: Blinds & Rounds) include payout calculators and structure generators but no rules reference — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1047302196); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/blindclock/6775924424); [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6738396271)
- PokerNow's only rule-shaped features are per-table option toggles (straddle, run it twice, bomb pot, 7-2 bounty) — [pokernow.com](https://www.pokernow.com/)

### Inferences
- The nearest thing to a "rules feature" in a management app is the per-table options list: it already encodes house rules as toggles (run it twice yes/no, straddle yes/no). A house-rules editor is a natural extension of that pattern, and no physical-table ledger app has built even that.
- Because the cohort is young (months old, near-zero ratings), a rules/ruling feature inside a ledger app would be a first in the category rather than a catch-up.

### Gaps
- Splitwise and general settle-up apps were not individually checked for any games/etiquette feature; none is known and none surfaced.
- Android-only home-game apps (if any) were not reached.

## Gap analysis: what nobody does well, what repeats in reviews, and what a rules/ruling feature would need to do to be different

### Takeaway
The field is empty where the home cash game lives: rules apps are tournament-only or cheat sheets, home-game apps have no rules at all, and the single AI ruling tool is institutional, iPad-first and a rules version behind. A ruling feature inside a ledger app can be different by being cash-game and house-rule aware, by citing the rule and the reasoning, by working offline at the table, and by producing a shareable, timestamped ruling that is attached to the night's record.

### Cited Findings (what the evidence above establishes)
- No app covers cash-game procedure: both TDA products are built on the TDA tournament rulebook — [Apple App Store TDA Rules](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6738895825); [Apple App Store TD Assistant](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6776635266)
- No app lets a group record its own house rules; the closest are PokerNow's table toggles — [pokernow.com](https://www.pokernow.com/)
- Home hosts resolve rulings by reaching for RRoP/TDA PDFs, custom rulebooks "reorganized for searchability", or forum consensus, with no app in the loop — [PokerChipForum thread](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/threads/home-game-ruling.42199/latest)
- The ruling authority at a home game is the host, and the problem case is when the host is in the hand — the forum thread's dispute arose exactly because "the hosts were both in the hand and disagreed" — [PokerChipForum thread](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/threads/home-game-ruling.42199/latest); [PokerNews: House Rules](https://www.pokernews.com/pokerterms/house-rules.htm)
- Answer-without-explanation is the content complaint that exists — [Poker Hands Quiz reviews](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1547131406)
- Trust in the host/app is the recurring complaint on the largest home-game client — [Pokerrrr 2 reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id592081067)
- Offline and no-ads are the features rules apps advertise — [Poker Hands - Cheat Sheet](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6757372869); [Poker Hands Quiz](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1547131406)
- The one AI ruling tool ships "verify against the rulebook" as a disclaimer and answers from 2024 rules while 2026 rules exist — [Apple App Store TD Assistant](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6776635266); [pokertda.com](https://www.pokertda.com/)
- The rules text itself is free and copyright-restricted for commercial reuse (RRoP) — [pagat.com RRoP v11](https://Pagat.Com/docs/RobsPkrRules11.pdf)
- Adjacent-domain referee bots differentiate on citing the exact rule passage and on asking for missing facts rather than guessing — [Ludus Judge AI](https://www.producthunt.com/products/ludus-judge-ai); [WotC patent coverage](https://draftsim.com/mtg-ai-assistant-wotc/)

### Inferences (what "different" would have to mean)
- **Cash-game first.** Table stakes, going south, rebuy minimums, "chips in play", leaving with chips, time-outs, splitting the pot by agreement, misdeals and exposed cards in a dealer's-choice home game — none of this is in the TDA rulebook the existing tools use. This alone would be unoccupied ground.
- **House rules as data, not prose.** The group's standing decisions (does a single chip over the bet count as a raise? run it twice allowed? can a player go south?) stored once per group and applied to every ruling. PokerNow proved toggles are the natural shape; nobody has done it for a physical table.
- **Interactive walkthrough, not chat.** A short decision tree ("Was 'raise' said? Was the chip larger than the bet? Had anyone acted after?") produces a deterministic answer with the rule number and the reasoning, which answers the "no explanations" complaint and avoids the invented-rule failure that the board-game referee bots exist to fix. An AI layer, if any, should map the player's description onto that tree rather than free-generate a verdict.
- **A shareable, timestamped ruling attached to the night.** The forum dispute was unresolvable because the hosts were in the hand; a ruling that is recorded in the night's ledger (who asked, what the facts were, what rule applied, who agreed) is the trust mechanism Pokerrrr 2 reviewers are asking for in a different form, and it is what a ledger app uniquely can do.
- **Offline.** Rulings happen at a kitchen table; the cheat-sheet apps advertise offline for the same reason.
- **Keep the rules version visible.** The TDA assistant's drift from 2024 to a 2026 rulebook is the maintenance failure to avoid: show the rules edition and date on every ruling.
- **Licensing.** RRoP cannot be reproduced commercially without consent; the rules content would need to be written in-house (or licensed), which is also what makes it customisable.
- **Build-or-add.** Nothing found is a standalone rules app with an audience (0–22 ratings across the category; the TDA assistant has none), while the paying behaviour that exists is for running the night. On this evidence a ruling feature is an add to the ledger, not a separate app: the ledger already knows the players, the stakes and the host, and a standalone rules app would have to acquire its users from a store category nobody searches.

### Gaps
- Reddit and the TDA forum could not be read, so the frequency of specific home-game ruling questions (which situations come up most) is not quantified here.
- Download numbers for every standalone rules app are missing; "nobody uses them" is inferred from rating counts, not installs.
- No accuracy evaluation of Poker TD Assistant or of poker GPTs on ruling questions was found; the claim that they are weak on rulings is an inference from the strategy-only research and the board-game precedents.
